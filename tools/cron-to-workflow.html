    Cron vs. an Unmeshed schedule

    Cron is great at one thing: starting a command at a time. Everything after that is up to you. Here's what changes when the same schedule runs as a workflow.

    Cron / crontabUnmeshed schedule
    Knows if a job failedNo, unless you add loggingYes, every run has a status
    RetriesWrite your ownBuilt-in error policies
    AlertsEmail from MAILTO, if configuredSlack, Teams or any integration as a step
    Run history & replayGrep the logsStep-level history and replay
    Dependencies between jobsGuess a time gapCross-process dependencies
    Runs across many serversOne crontab per machineAgents on Linux, macOS, Windows, IBM i
    Granularity1 minute1 second

    Cron syntax cheat sheet

    FieldAllowed valuesExample
    Minute0–59*/15 every 15 minutes
    Hour0–239-17 9am to 5pm
    Day of month1–311,15 the 1st and 15th
    Month1–12 or JAN–DEC*/3 every quarter
    Day of week0–7 or SUN–SAT (0 and 7 are Sunday)MON-FRI weekdays

    * means every value, , separates a list, - makes a range and / sets a step. When both day of month and day of week are restricted, standard cron runs the job when either one matches.

    Common questions

    Does this tool send my cron expression anywhere?
    No. Everything runs in your browser.

    Why are my next run times different on the server?
    Cron uses the server's time zone. Switch the time zone above to UTC if your server runs on UTC, which most cloud servers do.

    How do I move a cron job to Unmeshed?
    Create a schedule with the same expression and point it at a workflow. For shell scripts, install an Unmeshed agent on the server and run the command as a step. The scheduled jobs guide walks through it.
